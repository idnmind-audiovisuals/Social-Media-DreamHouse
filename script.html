Dream House · The Trailer

Voice Over & Script

Two parts of one film. The voiceover carries the inner memory of the experience; the script will translate that emotional journey into image, action, rhythm, and sound.

Part 01

Dream House Voice Over

Length2–3 minutes
StyleCinematic art-film trailer
Emotional arc

The film begins like a dream the viewer cannot fully understand. It gradually becomes warmer, more human, and more alive. By the end, the audience realizes that what initially seemed imagined has become real.

Wonder→Arrival→Belonging→Creation→Transformation→Expansion
Voice direction

The voice should feel intimate and reflective—as though someone is remembering the experience, not narrating or explaining it. Leave generous silence between passages. The delivery is vulnerable, curious, and slightly dreamlike.

Part 02

The visual script to be confirmed.

Dream House
Format2–3-minute cinematic art-film trailer
Emotional arcWonder → Arrival → Belonging → Creation → Transformation → Expansion

The film follows one person moving from curiosity and distance into the lived world of Dream House.

At first, the experience appears only in fragments—almost like memories from a dream they have not yet lived. As they arrive, the film becomes warmer, more intimate, and more embodied. By the end, the dreams introduced at the beginning have become tangible, and the protagonist returns to the world carrying something new.

The film should not explain Dream House completely. It should allow the viewer to feel it.

Visual Direction

The image should feel like reality slipping into memory.

Opening

  • Cooler and more neutral
  • Observational
  • Slight emotional distance
  • Reflections and partial views
  • Fast flashes that cannot yet be understood
  • Controlled halation around Lisbon lights and windows

Middle

  • Warmer skin tones
  • Greater camera proximity
  • Handheld intimacy
  • More movement
  • Longer reactions after key moments
  • Rich practical light
  • Increased glow and bloom

Ending

  • Expansive
  • Luminous
  • Emotionally explosive, followed by stillness
  • Strong highlight halation
  • Dreamlike overexposure
  • A sense that the experience is moving beyond the physical house

Use imperfections intentionally

  • Focus breathing
  • Reflections
  • Foreground obstructions
  • Lens flare
  • Slow shutter
  • Motion blur
  • Film grain
  • Blown highlights
  • Frames that begin before the action and continue after it ends

Sound Direction

The film should not depend only on music and narration.

Use diegetic sound and immersive foley to make the viewer feel physically present inside the experience.

Key Sounds

  • Breathing
  • Curtains opening
  • Lisbon tram bells
  • Wheels moving over tram tracks
  • Doors opening
  • Footsteps
  • Bare feet on wood
  • Wind through a car window
  • Tea being poured
  • Marker strokes
  • Keyboard clicks
  • Camera buttons
  • Fabric moving
  • Ocean water
  • Distant conversations
  • Fragments of laughter
  • Celebration
  • A project notification
  • The silence immediately after a breakthrough

Allow the music to stop completely at least twice:

  1. After the initial fast-cut hook
  2. Before or during the final manifestation sequence

The silence should make the spoken fragments feel like thoughts appearing inside the viewer rather than lines being delivered to them.

The final emotional response should be:

“I don’t completely understand what happened there… but I need to experience it.”
